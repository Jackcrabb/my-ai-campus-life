# Humanities, arts, PE and enquiry question alignment

Checked: 10 October 2026. Authoring source: [grade-items-humanities.json](../scripts/content-authoring/grade-items-humanities.json).

This file documents **108 original short questions: six spaces × six school years × three levels**. Every question has a separate problem, three options, specific feedback for each option and a learning takeaway. These are supplementary learning checks, not copied examination questions, an official question bank, a complete subject syllabus or evidence that a student has mastered a subject. No past-paper question, textbook passage or official marking scheme was reproduced. Invented historical excerpts and numerical case data are identified as fictional or teaching models in the relevant questions.

The S1–S6 placements are the project's instructional sequence. EDB key-stage documents do not prescribe every one of these concepts in that precise year. Teachers should check prior knowledge and adjust placement, especially for senior Music, Visual Arts and PE concepts. A text multiple-choice task cannot assess practical performance, a complete artwork, physical competence or an extended historical argument.

## Official references and limits of alignment

### History (`history`)

The [EDB History Curriculum Guide S1–S3, 2019](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/pshe/Hist_Curr_Guide_S1-3_Eng_final_10072019.pdf) gives a chronological junior sequence: ancient, modern and contemporary worlds. The first nine items follow that broad progression while practising chronology, interpretation and causation. Senior items develop the source comparison and qualified argument skills described in the [2026 HKEAA History assessment framework](https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/DocLibrary/HKDSE/Subject_Information/hist/2026hkdse-e-hist.pdf); the question format here is deliberately a short learning check, not an imitation of its examination papers. A direct fetch of the 2027 subject PDF returned HTTP 403 during this check, so it is not presented as reviewed.

[EDB's renewal index](https://www.edb.gov.hk/en/curriculum-development/renewal/all.html) dates the optimised senior History/Chinese History frameworks to October 2025. The [2026–27 EDB budget reply](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/about-edb/press/legco/replies-to-fc/edb-e-2627.pdf) identifies planned S4 implementation in 2027/28; the [HKEAA review index](https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/en/HKDSE/nss_review/) identifies History/Chinese History assessment changes for 2030 and beyond. These future arrangements are not automatically attributed to all current S4–S6 cohorts. Chinese History and History remain distinct subjects; this shared game room does not claim to cover the full Chinese History curriculum.

### Visual Arts (`art`)

The [EDB Arts curriculum index](https://www.edb.gov.hk/en/curriculum-development/kla/arts-edu/curriculum-docs/index.html) lists the 2024 P1–S6 Visual Arts guide and the [senior CDC–HKEAA Visual Arts C&A guide, updated 2015](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/arts-edu/curriculum-docs/VA_CAGuide_e_2015.pdf). Items connect visual elements, materials and communication with criticism, context, making, experimentation and reflection. Senior items are enrichment relevant to arts learning and selected elective concepts; they do not assume every student takes HKDSE Visual Arts. Questions assess a stated purpose or supported interpretation, not a universal ranking of artistic taste.

### Music (`music`)

The same [EDB index](https://www.edb.gov.hk/en/curriculum-development/kla/arts-edu/curriculum-docs/index.html) lists the 2024 general Music guide. The [senior CDC–HKEAA Music C&A guide, September 2024](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/arts-edu/curriculum-docs/mus_c_and_a_e_2024.pdf) applies from S4 in 2024/25 and the 2027 HKDSE. Items progress from pulse and note values to texture, tonal relationships, composing decisions and performance/recording analysis. They support understanding for listening, performing and creating, but the present questions use text and notation names: they do not constitute an aural or practical examination.

### Physical Education (`playground`)

The [EDB PE curriculum index](https://www.edb.gov.hk/en/curriculum-development/kla/pe/curriculum-doc/index.html), [six-strand learning-topic overview](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/pe/curriculum-doc/scope_of_learning_pri/F_LT_SS_eng.pdf), and [CDC–HKEAA PE C&A guide, July 2023 update](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/pe/curriculum-doc/PE%20C%26A%20Guide_e.pdf) support connections among movement, tactics, fitness knowledge, learning, assessment and participation. The senior guide distinguishes General PE from the PE elective and applies from S4 in 2023/24. Selected senior theoretical concepts here are enrichment, not a claim that all students take the elective. The tasks analyse teaching examples, not personalised exercise prescriptions, medical advice or substitutes for teacher-supervised practical learning.

### Library enquiry (`library`)

This space represents **interdisciplinary information literacy**, not a separate compulsory HKDSE subject. The [EDB Information Literacy learning framework, 2024](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/edu-system/primary-secondary/applicable-to-primary-secondary/it-in-edu/Information-Literacy/IL_learningFramework/IL_LearningFramework%282024%29_EN.pdf) informs a progression from finding and evaluating information to organising, synthesising and communicating evidence. Topics span catalogue use, search strategies, citation, methods and transparent synthesis. Grade placement within each key stage is an editorial teaching decision.

### Hall civic/economic enquiry (`hall`)

[EDB Circular 20/2024](https://applications.edb.gov.hk/circular/upload/EDBC/EDBC24020E.pdf) identifies **Citizenship, Economics and Society (S1–S3)** as a junior subject, introduced from S1 in 2024/25, including personal/social development, resources/economic activities and social systems/citizenship. Junior items practise selected resource, consumer and participation concepts; they do not cover all its required modules. **S4–S6 hall items are interdisciplinary civic/economic enquiry enrichment**. They are not labelled the junior subject's senior syllabus, complete Citizenship and Social Development coverage, or HKDSE Economics preparation. Their evidence, participation and resource-allocation skills also connect to the [EDB seven secondary learning goals](https://www.edb.gov.hk/en/curriculum-development/7-learning-goals/secondary/index.html).

## Authoring, language and validation

- The compact authoring format stores original Traditional Chinese and English together. The integration owner's existing OpenCC `t2s` build step generates Simplified Chinese; this file does not add a runtime language dependency. Terminology and meaning must remain aligned after generation. No claim of independent native-speaker review is made.
- The first option is the canonical correct answer in this source only. The integration generator owns deterministic balanced rotation and final option IDs; answer correctness must move with the option. The delivered game must not expose a fixed correct position.
- Source checks establish curricular connections and dates, not official endorsement or a full syllabus match. All classroom quantities, participant results and historical teaching extracts are illustrative. No actual pupil data is used.
- Local authoring checks verify 108 unique subject/year/level combinations, 18 unique question texts per room, three distinct options, complete bilingual fields, and specific option feedback. Numerical results were also checked independently: the budget, unit prices, six-month costs, decimals, speed and musical durations/intervals.
- Runtime generation, full trilingual validation and integration tests belong to the coordinating authoring task. Real student trials, empirical item difficulty and teaching efficacy remain unverified. Teachers should review and pilot before using scores for consequential assessment.
